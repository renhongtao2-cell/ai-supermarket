⚖️ Free Tier Comparison — Like-for-Like, Not a List

A flat list of free allowances is not a comparison. 500 credits and 500 minutes are not the same thing, and a one-off grant is not a monthly reset. So we grouped the 36 allowances we recorded by what the unit actually measures, then split each group by how it renews.

Machine-readable index: /llms.txt.

Why grouping by unit is the whole point

The most common mistake when reading free-tier tables is comparing the numbers. A tool offering "10,000 credits" looks ten times more generous than one offering "1,000 credits" — until you find out that the first tool charges 200 credits per generation and the second charges 5. The unit is vendor-invented, and it is deliberately not standardised, because a bespoke unit makes it harder to compare against a competitor.

Where a unit is objective — minutes of audio, words of text, API requests — the comparison is real and you can trust it. Where it is vendor-defined — credits, points, tokens — the number tells you almost nothing on its own, and the honest thing to do is show it next to its family so at least the incomparability is visible rather than hidden.

So this page does not rank the 36 tools into a single "best free tier" list, because that ranking would be meaningless. It groups them so that the only rows sitting side by side are ones that can actually be judged against each other.

The second axis: does it come back?

How an allowance renews matters more than how large it is. A small monthly allowance supports a habit; a large one-off grant supports a single project and then leaves you stranded. Of the 36 allowances recorded here, 20 reset on a schedule (monthly or daily), 6 are one-off or non-expiring grants, and 2 are trial-only.

The one-line rule

If you intend to use a tool continuously, only the 20 allowances that reset are candidates. A one-off grant is a demo with extra steps, however large the number looks.

What this page deliberately does not do

  • It does not declare a winner. A winner across incompatible units would be an invention, not a finding.
  • It does not normalise the numbers. Multiplying a daily allowance by thirty to manufacture a monthly figure would put a number on the page that no vendor ever published.
  • It does not fill the gaps. Tools whose vendors state a free plan but publish no figure are listed on the free-tier page without a number, rather than with a guess.
  • It does not carry a "commercial use" column. That answer is per-plan and per-jurisdiction, and reading it off a marketing page reliably produces errors. Check the licence attached to the plan you are actually on.

Before you rely on any of these numbers

Free tiers are the part of a pricing page most likely to change without notice, because they are a marketing lever rather than a revenue line. Every figure here was read from the vendor's own pricing page and reviewed 2026-09-18; several were re-fetched to confirm, because cached copies go stale. Treat this as a starting point for your own check, not as a contract — and when a number decides your choice, open the vendor's pricing page yourself before you commit.
